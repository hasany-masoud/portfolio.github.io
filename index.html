About

I am a materials scientist and researcher at DTU Construct, working at the intersection of materials science, chemistry, nanobiotechnology, and biomedical engineering. My research focuses on designing functional and sustainable materials with applications ranging from biomaterials and tissue engineering to flexible electronics, energy storage, sensing, and advanced manufacturing.

I am particularly interested in understanding how the chemistry, structure, and interfaces of materials can be engineered to achieve new functionalities and improved performance. Throughout my career, I have worked across disciplines and collaborated with researchers from academia, healthcare, and industry, combining material design, experimental characterization, and application-driven research.

I am passionate about translating fundamental scientific ideas into practical technologies and creating collaborations that bridge the gap between materials research and real-world applications. Previously, I was a Marie Skłodowska-Curie postdoctoral fellow at DTU and a postdoctoral researcher at Geneva University Hospitals. I am co-applicant in a DFF Green Transition grant (7.2 million DKK, 2025).

Current Projects

Active · DFF Green Transition 2025

Sustainable Hydrogel Materials for Green Technology Applications

Co-leading a 7.2 million DKK DFF-funded project developing sustainable, functional hydrogel-based materials for green technology. Focuses on environmentally responsible material design without compromising performance in energy and sensing applications.

Green Materials Sustainability Hydrogels Energy
Active

Wearable Hydrogel Electronics for Health Monitoring

Designing ultra-stretchable, super-tough, ion-doped hydrogels that function simultaneously as structural, sensing, and conducting elements in wearable platforms — from smart socks for edema monitoring to wireless human motion sensing systems.

Ionic Hydrogels Strain Sensors Human Motion Soft Robotics
Active

Hydrogel Electrolytes for Flexible Energy Storage

Engineering atmosphere-stable, anti-fire hydrogel electrolytes using hygroscopic ion-doping strategies. Achieves super-tough, freeze-resistant behaviour suitable for next-generation wearable batteries and supercapacitors operating in real-world conditions.

Electrolytes Energy Storage Li-ion Doping Flexible Devices
Active

Bioadhesive & Self-Healing Hydrogels for Tissue Repair

Developing bioadhesive, self-healing hydrogel platforms with tunable chemical functionality for chronic wound care and tissue engineering. Integrates diverse crosslinking chemistries and nanocomposite reinforcement for clinically translatable wound dressings and scaffolds.

Bioadhesion Self-Healing Wound Care Tissue Engineering
Active

Injectable Hydrogels for Bone Augmentation

Combining organic and inorganic phases in tough, injectable, self-setting cement-based hydrogels for minimally invasive bone augmentation. Validated in vitro and ex vivo as a strategy for noninvasive orthopaedic repair without open surgery.

Injectable Systems Bone Repair Cement Hydrogels Orthopaedics
Completed · MSCA Fellowship

Silk-Graphene Hydrogels for Unbreakable Bionics

Developed multifunctional, self-healing silk-graphene hydrogels enabling the manufacture of unbreakable bionic devices. The material platform demonstrated direct implications for soft robotics, human–machine interfaces, and 3D-printed electronics.
